# Methodology

The experiment series used four recurring rules:

1. Freeze the question and contract before measured calls whenever possible.
2. Preserve exact repository lineage and avoid production mutation during research.
3. Treat deterministic restart parity as a hard correctness requirement.
4. Escalate baselines after positive results rather than stopping at weak controls.

Baseline pressure increased from recent/full-history prompting to same-count
order-blind controls, full-history semantic reduction, supervised deterministic
reducers, compact relationship alternatives, cross-family transfer, and a
prospective third-family holdout.

Stages 7–11 also exposed an evaluator boundary: hidden behavior labels can be
hard to reverse-infer from natural dialogue even when the dialogue faithfully
executes the selected intent. The series therefore separated host-selected
behavior from surface realization.

In replacement tests, CMI state is the reference label. A baseline mismatch
means the baseline failed to reproduce CMI state; it does not prove that CMI is
objectively more human-correct.

Stage 18 isolates downstream consequence by holding emotion, interpretation,
attention, continuity state, and the host policy constant while substituting
only the relationship-state source.
